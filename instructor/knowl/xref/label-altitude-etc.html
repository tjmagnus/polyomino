Exercise 3.

Triangle \(\Delta ABC\text{,}\) trapezoid \(DEGF\text{,}\) and parallelogram \(HKJI\) are provided in Figure 1.2.28.
🔗

(a)

Label a side of triangle \(\Delta ABC\) as the base. Then draw and label the altitude of the triangle corresponding to that base.
🔗
Hint.
Any of the three sides of a triangle can be chosen as the base. Once chosen, draw a perpendicular line segment from that base to the opposite vertex.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Possible Answer:
🔗
Let \(\overline{BC}\) be the base of triangle \(\Delta ABC\text{.}\) Draw the altitude as a line segment from point \(A\) perpendicular to \(\overline{BC}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Step-By-Step Explanation For Drawing:
🔗
Choose \(\overline{BC}\) as the base of triangle \(\Delta ABC\text{.}\) From point \(A\text{,}\) draw a perpendicular line segment to \(\overline{BC}\text{.}\) Label this line segment as the altitude. This altitude represents the height of the triangle from vertex \(A\) to base \(\overline{BC}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Which two sides of trapezoid \(DEGF\) could be considered to be bases? Label one as the base. Also label the summit and legs of the trapezoid. Then draw and label an altitude for \(DEGF\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
A trapezoid has one pair of parallel sides, which are the bases. The altitude is the perpendicular distance between these bases.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Bases: \(\overline{DE}\) and \(\overline{FG}\) (since they are parallel to each other). One of these will be a base and the other will be a summit.
🔗
Legs: \(\overline{DF}\) and \(\overline{EG}\text{.}\)
🔗
Altitude (possible answer): Line segment drawn from point \(E\) to \(\overline{FG}\text{.}\) Should be perpendicular to \(\overline{FG}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Step-By-Step Explanation For Drawing:
🔗
Trapezoid \(DEGF\) has sides \(\overline{DE}\) and \(\overline{FG}\) that are parallel so these are the bases. The non-parallel sides \(\overline{DF}\) and \(\overline{EG}\) are the legs. Choose base \(\overline{FG}\) and a point opposite on \(\overline{DE}\) (example: point \(E\)). Draw a line segment from point \(E\) that is perpendicular to \(\overline{FG}\text{.}\) Label this line segment as the altitude. The altitude represents the distance between the bases.
🔗

(c)

Label side \(\overline{HI}\) as the base of parallelogram \(HIJK\text{.}\) Sketch and label an altitude for \(HIJK\) corresponding to base \(\overline{HI}\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
In a parallelogram, opposite sides are parallel and equal. Draw a line segment that is perpendicular to both the base (side \(\overline{HI}\)) and its opposite side.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Let \(\overline{HI}\) be the base. The altitude is a perpendicular line segment from either point \(K\) or point \(J\) to side \(\overline{HI}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Label \(\overline{HI}\) as the base. Draw a perpendicular line segment from either point \(K\) or point \(J\) to the base \(\overline{HI}\text{.}\) This line segment is the altitude, representing the height corresponding to base \(\overline{HI}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(d)

Could another side of \(HIJK\) been chosen as the base? Which one(s)? Would the altitude be the same?
🔗
Hint.
In a parallelogram, there are two pairs of opposite sides. How does the choice of a base pair affect the altitude (height) of a parallelogram?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Yes, any of the four sides of parallelogram \(HIJK\) can be chosen as the base. The altitude will vary depending on which base is chosen.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Yes, another side of \(HIJK\) could be chosen as the base. If \(\overline{HI}\) is a base, then its opposite side \(\overline{JK}\) is a base. Alternatively, if \(\overline{HK}\) and \(\overline{IJ}\) could be used as the other pair of bases.
🔗
The altitude is the perpendicular distance between opposite sides. The altitude is the same within each base pair (example: \(\overline{HI}\) and \(\overline{JK}\)). However, the altitude is not the same between different base pairs (example: \(\overline{HI}\) and If \(\overline{IJ}\)).
