Exploration 1.1.1. Tiling Rectangles with Polyominoes.

This task simply asks you to fill a box, but you are encouraged to think outside the box as you do this activity. Take chances and try out different strategies as the challenge increases. Take time to reflect as you respond to the "why" questions and be prepared to share your ideas with your classmates. Have fun!
🔗

(a)

Just as a domino consists of two squares joined at an edge, a tetromino is a flat figure created by joining four unit squares
 1 
A unit square is one unit high and one unit wide.
along edges. Fill the blue square with non-overlapping copies of the green L-shaped tetronimo. How many tetrominoes are required?

To use the interactive tool, click and drag the L-shape to create a new copy of the shape. Use the large dot, to rotate the copy. Arrange copies to cover the blue square. This activity may also be done on paper using a \(4 \times 4\) grid and an L-tile formed from four unit squares.

(b)

Pictured below are the four other tetrominoes: an I-shape, a Z-shape, a T-shape, and a small square. Like the L-shape, some of these can on their own cover the larger square without overlaps or gaps. Identify which tetrominoes will tile one of the larger squares and show how this can be done. If there are any tetrominoes which cannot tile the square alone, explain why you believe the tiling is impossible.
🔗
Once again in the application, copies of the I-shape, Z-shape, T-shape, and small square can be created by clicking the shape and dragging. Four larger squares are provided; try to tile one square grid with the I-shape, one with the Z-shape, etc.

(d)

Similarly, we define a pentomino to be a figure formed by joining five congruent squares along edges. One pentomino is pictured. Create new pentominoes by copying and moving the provided purple unit square. Note that if two pentominoes are reflections or rotations of each other, we consider them to be the same pentomino. Be sure to save a copy of your sketch for future reference.
