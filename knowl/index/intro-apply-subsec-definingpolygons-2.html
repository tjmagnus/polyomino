In the first part of this course, we will work primarily with polygons. Stating a clear, clean definition of the word polygon is difficult so we will describe it verbally and give several examples. If you search for a definition in books or on the web, you will find different attempts to give a clear definition. Here, we describe a polygon as being a closed plane figure formed by a sequence of straight sides (or line segments). Being a plane figure means that each polygon is a shape that lies in the plane or on a flat surface. By closed, we mean that a polygon will have a well-defined interior; the polygon forms a solid boundary between this interior and the rest of the plane. In addition, each vertex (or corner) of the polygon will be an endpoint for both of the sides that meet at that point, no two sides will intersect in between their endpoints, and exactly two sides will meet at any vertex.
